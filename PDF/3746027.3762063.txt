Abstract
Depression detection remains challenged by generalized modeling
approaches that fail to account for individual heterogeneity. To ad-
dress this, the Multimodal Personality-aware Depression Detection
(MPDD) Challenge introduced personalized features into the model-
ing process, aiming to better capture individual variability. However,
the baseline models still exhibit two critical limitations: the neglect
of textual semantics embedded in audio, and inconsistent predic-
tions for the same subject across tasks and samples. Motivated
by these limitations, we introduce HOPE (Hierarchical fusion for
Optimized and Personality-aware Estimation of Depression), a uni-
fied framework for consistent, subject-level depression estimation.
HOPE first employs a Latent Semantic Projection (LSP) module to
reconstruct textual semantics from audio features when transcripts
are unavailable. It then introduces a consistency-aware integration
mechanism that hierarchically fuses multi-branch predictions to
resolve inter-task and inter-sample contradictions. HOPE achieved
first place in the MPDD Challenge Young Track, demonstrating

1 Introduction
Depression is a mental health disorder and has emerged as a major
public health concern worldwide [ 15,22]. This disorder not only
severely impairs an individual’s quality of life and social function-
ing but also poses long-term risks to their physical health, family
relationships, and professional development [ 16,19]. Traditionally,
depression assessment methods have mainly relied on psychometric
scales [ 1,8] and shallow learning algorithms applied to structured
data [ 4,21]. In recent years, the rapid progress of deep learning has
opened new directions for depression detection, especially through
multimodal approaches that integrate information from speech,
facial expressions, and text data [2, 6, 24].
However, most existing approaches still adopt a generalized mod-
eling paradigm which often fail to account for the heterogeneity
of depression across individuals [ 13,18]. For instance, even at the
same level of depression severity, people with different personality

To highlight these limitations, the ACM Multimedia 2025 MPDD
Challenge [ 5] introduced a benchmark task based on pre-extracted
multimodal features. While the baseline MPDD models have vali-
dated the critical role of personality traits in depression modeling
and demonstrated that incorporating personality information sig-

First, the baseline models ignore the textual semantic content con-
veyed by the audio (i.e., the actual verbal expressions of the subjects),
which often contains crucial clinical cues for identifying depressive
tendencies. Second, in the MPDD dataset, each subject is associ-
ated with three distinct samples, each evaluated under two window
lengths (1s and 5s) and two classification tasks (binary and ternary).
In the baseline setting, these are treated as independent instances,
disregarding subject identity. As a result, existing methods often
produce inconsistent and contradictory clinical predictions across
samples, window lengths, and tasks for the same individual.
To address these limitations, we propose HOPE—Hierarchical
fusion for Optimized and Personality-aware Estimation of
depression, a unified framework tailored for consistency-aware,
subject-level depression prediction. The innovations of this study
are as follows: first, inspired by the observation that textual infor-
mation in audio-visual dialogues can provide richer text seman-
tic features [ 17,19], we construct a novel LSP module trained onextensive external dataset. In scenarios where raw dialogue tran-
scripts are unavailable and access to original audio-visual data is
restricted, this module reconstructs textual semantic representa-
tions from audio features, effectively supplementing critical text-
based semantic information. Second, to address the subject-level
prediction inconsistency observed in the MPDD baseline, we de-
sign a consistency-aware individual-level integration mechanism.
By hierarchically fusing outputs from multiple task-specific and
sample-specific branches, the model learns to resolve logical con-
tradictions and amplify the most reliable signals, ensuring coherent
and clinically plausible subject-level predictions. The main contri-
butions of this study can be summarized as follows:
•We propose the LSP module, which extracts transcript-level
semantic information from audio features when the raw
dialogue text data and audio-visual data are inaccessible,
thereby supplementing critical text-based semantic informa-
tion carried by the audio.
•We propose a consistency-aware individual-level integration
mechanism that hierarchically fuses multi-branch predic-
tions to resolve inter-task and inter-sample contradictions in
the baseline of MPDD, thereby enhancing the subject-level
reliability of depression assessment.
•Our proposed HOPE framework secured first place in the
MPDD-Young competition track, demonstrating superior
cross-modal learning capabilities under strict privacy con-
straints (only pre-extracted features were permitted) and
limited training samples.

2 Method
2.1 Method Overview
To address two limitations of the baseline which neglect audio’s
textual semantics (containing clinically vital verbal expressions)
and which yield inconsistent judgments by processing same-subject
samples independently, we propose HOPE. As shown in Fig. 1, its
framework contains LSP Module and consistency-aware individual-
level integration mechanism , specifically addressing these issues.
Inspired by evidence that textual modalities provide richer clini-
cal cues than audio-visual data for depression detection [ 17,19], we
trained the LSP Module on the auxiliary MER2025 dataset [ 10] to es-
tablish end-to-end mapping from audio to textual features—enabling
direct semantic extraction without speech transcripts [ 9]. The re-
sulting "Pseudo-Text Features" are incorporated as a new modality
into our framework, with technical implementations detailed in
Section 2.2.
While preserving the four-branch subtask structure defined by
the MPDD challenge, HOPE builds on the baseline design where
separate feature extractors are used for each window length (1s,
5s) and task type (binary, ternary), forming four distinct branches.
Each subject contributes 12 inputs in total—three samples under
two window lengths and two task settings. However, the baseline
treats these inputs independently, often yielding conflicting predic-
tions across tasks and temporal windows for the same subject. To
resolve this, we introduce a two-stage hierarchical fusion mecha-
nism that promotes subject-level consistency. Sample outputs are
first aggregated into task-level embeddings via learned attention,
then integrated through a temporal reasoning module that rec-
onciles discrepancies across branches. This allows the model to
selectively amplify reliable predictions, producing coherent and
clinically meaningful outcomes at the subject level. Further techni-
cal details are elaborated in Section 2.3, and Section 2.4.
2.2 Latent Semantic Projection Module
In addition to leveraging static personality traits, HOPE introduces a
dynamic semantic representation derived from the subject’s spoken
audio. This representation is designed to encode the high-level
linguistic meaning—complementing what it was said (content) and
by whom (personality). The goal is to project each audio segment
into a semantic space aligned with sentence-level language models,
enabling deeper understanding of depression-relevant content.
Due to privacy protection constraints in the MPDD, only pre-
extracted Wav2Vec [ 20] features are available instead of raw audio
data. To extract richer semantic representations from these limited
audio features that better capture textual content, as shown in Fig 2,
we design a pooled mapping approach that transforms the available
512-dimensional audio features into a text-aligned semantic space
of 1024 dimensions.

training strategy that treats the entire utterance as a unit during
training while applying the learned mapping segment-wise during
inference. Specifically, during the training phase, we average-pool
the full Wav2Vec feature sequence from each utterance to produce
a global 512-dimensional representation. This pooled feature is

2.3 Consistency-aware Individual-level Feature
Integration
Building upon the MPDD baseline, each of the four branches in
our HOPE framework corresponds to a distinct temporal resolution
and classification task. To enhance feature quality under the strict
input constraints of the challenge, we introduce three lightweight
and effective modifications: (i) convolutional layers to capture lo-
cal audio–visual patterns, (ii) a Q-Former module for cross-modal
alignment between audio and visual features, and (iii) a personality-
aware attention mechanism that injects subject-specific priors into
sample-level representations.
These enriched embeddings are used to derive a stable and con-
sistent representation for each subject through a two-stage fusion
strategy, which integrates features across both subtasks and tem-
poral samples in a 12 →3→1 pattern. This layered integration
enables the model to consolidate heterogeneous cues into a co-
herent individual-level semantic representation. Unlike conven-
tional attention-based fusion that assumes feature compatibility,
our approach shifts the paradigm from feature aggregation to
conflict arbitration, explicitly addressing cross-task prediction
conflicts through personality-aware resolution.

task-specific classifiers to focus on different severity granularity.

3 Experiment
3.1 Dataset and metrics
LSP module dataset and metrics. To train and evaluate the pro-
posed LSP module, we utilize the MER2025 dataset—a large-scale
multimodal dataset developed for emotion recognition in real-world
Chinese media content [ 10]. The full dataset contains more than
180,000 utterances, each annotated with corresponding emotional
labels and aligned across audio, text, and video modalities. For our
purposes, we focus exclusively on paired audio-text samples, select-
ing 100,000 utterances for training the LSP module. These are split
into training, validation, and test sets in a 7:1.5:1.5 ratio. To evaluate
the quality of the projected Pseudo-Text Features, we employ Root
Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Cosine Similarity (Cosine Sim) as
complementary metrics.
Full model dataset and metrics. We evaluate our full model
on the MPDD-Young dataset [ 5], a multimodal dataset designed
for depression detection in young adults. This dataset provides
synchronized audio, visual, and demographic data, alongside PHQ-
9-based depression severity labels and personality attributes based
on the BigFive-10 scale. Each sample is further enriched with gender,
age, and birth region metadata to support more nuanced analysis.
Instead of raw audio or video, the organizers provide pre-extracted
multimodal features for each participant. Each individual has three
data samples per modality (denoted as _1, _2, _3). The audio, visual,
and personalized semantic features used in our experiments follow
the configuration provided in the MPDD-Young dataset [5].
Following the baseline evaluation protocol, we adopt four met-
rics: weighted and unweighted F1 scores ( 𝑊𝐹1/𝑈𝐹1) as well as
weighted and unweighted accuracies ( 𝑊𝐴𝑐𝑐/𝑈𝐴𝑐𝑐). The final score
is computed as a weighted average of the F1 scores and accuracies
across the four tasks.
In our evaluation, each task𝑖in the above equations corresponds
to a specific classification setup supported by the dataset. The
MPDD-Young dataset defines two tasks: (1) binary classification
between normal anddepressed, and (2) ternary classification among
normal, mildly depressed, and severely depressed. Performance met-
rics are averaged across these tasks to compute the final track-level
score.

pre-extracted audio features and enables the capture of fine-grained
semantic cues over time. Such a representation is inherently com-
patible with our hierarchical fusion mechanism, which operates
across time and modality.
Full model experimental setup. Our full model’s experiments
adopt a two-stage training strategy: (1) The first stage focuses on
training each branch model to specialize in depression detection
for its specific input type—defined by a particular combination of
sample, window length, and classification task—thereby enhancing
task-specific performance; (2) The second stage builds upon the
trained branch models from the first stage, employing a hierarchi-
cal fusion mechanism that integrates task-specific features across
branches and temporal samples.
The first stage adopts a 10-fold cross-validation strategy follow-
ing the baseline configuration, where each of the four task-specific
branches is trained separately using parameters consistent with
the original setting. The second stage inherits the overall configu-
ration from the first stage—including data loading strategies and
random seed settings—and is trained for 100 epochs using the best-
performing fold’s weights. For all experiments, we use a batch
size of 8 and fix the random seed. The dataset is split by subject
IDs to prevent identity leakage between training and validation.
The network employs ReLU activations, with a dropout rate of 0.3
applied to the classifier layers. Optimization is conducted using
Adam (learning rate = 0.001, 𝛽1= 0.9, weight decay = 1e-4), and a
plateau-based scheduler reduces the learning rate when validation
accuracy stagnates. All models are implemented in PyTorch 2.3.0
and trained on 8×L20 GPUs.

4 Conclusion
In this work, we propose HOPE, a novel hierarchical multimodal
framework for depression estimation that explicitly incorporates
personality-aware representations and subject-level consistency
modeling. By introducing the LSP module, HOPE effectively bridges
the semantic gap between audio features and textual representa-
tions under privacy-constrained settings. The hierarchical fusion
strategy integrates heterogeneous modalities and sample-level pre-
dictions into coherent subject-level outputs. HOPE achieved 1st
place in the ACM MM 2025 MPDD Challenge (Young Track), demon-
strating outstand performance. Our findings underscore the impor-
tance of modeling individualized behaviors in multimodal affec-
tive computing and open avenues for future work on personality-
informed mental health assessment.
