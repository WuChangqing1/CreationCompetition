Abstract
Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is a prevalent and severe psychi-
atric disorder, and its detection remains challenging due to the com-
plexity and variability of its symptoms. Traditional single-modality
methods often fail to capture the full spectrum of depressive cues,
which has led to the rise of multimodal methods. The ACM Multi-
media 2025 "Multimodal Personality-Aware Depression Detection
Challenge" (MPDD 2025) aims to advance the development of more
accurate depression detection models by incorporating multimodal
data. In this paper, we proposed a Multi-Level Segment Fusion Based
on Adaptive Time-Window Selection (MSF-ATS) method for the
MPDD-Elderly Track. To address the challenge of sparse and tran-
sient depressive symptoms, we fuse segment-level classifications
to obtain subject-level classifications. An adaptive time-window
selection based on mean class variance is employed to choose the
window with the smallest variance for more stable detection results.
Our method achieved an average score of 0.8576 on the MPDD 2025

1 Introduction
Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is one of the most common and
deadliest psychiatric disorders worldwide, affecting hundreds of
millions of people across all age groups [ 10]. Determining whether
a person has depression and assessing its severity can effectively
control the condition and prevent further deterioration. However,
due to the complexity and variety of symptoms associated with
MDD, traditional single-modality methods often struggle to capture
the full range of depressive cues [ 3,4]. This limitation has led to

growing interest in multimodal methods for depression detection.
By integrating multiple data sources, such as audio, visual, and tex-
tual information, multimodal depression detection models capture
a broader range of depressive cues, thereby improving the overall
performance of depression detection tasks [2, 16, 23].
To accelerate the clinical translation of multimodal depression
detection methods, ACM Multimedia 2025 organized the "Multi-
modal Personality-Aware Depression Detection Challenge" (MPDD
2025) [ 7]. This competition innovatively introduces the dimension
of personality awareness by providing each participant with struc-
tured identity information including demographic attributes and
the Big Five personality traits, filling the gap in previous databases
where such data was missing. The competition comprises two
sub-tracks: the MPDD-Elderly Track, which targets the elderly
population with data from real clinical interview records and the
MPDD-Young Track, which focuses on younger subjects with data
derived from reading tasks and self-reported questionnaires. The
competition aims to encourage researchers to develop more accu-
rate multimodal depression detection models.
In the process of automated depression detection, how to appro-
priately segment the data into time windows is a key challenge. Re-
search has shown that the choice of data segments directly impacts
the performance of depression detection models [ 15]. Some studies
support using shorter segments to capture subtle and rapid changes
in depression signals [21]. However, short segments are more sus-
ceptible to noise interference, potentially leading the model to over-
fit irrelevant information, thus affecting detection performance. In
contrast, longer segments provide more contextual information,
helping to enhance the model’s stability and reduce sensitivity to
brief noise [ 5]. However, long time windows also have limitations,
as they may obscure some brief but crucial depressive signals. This
reflects the trade-off between granularity and robustness. How to
reasonably choose the time window for data remains a significant
challenge in current depression detection. Moreover, depressive
symptoms in clinical interviews are often not consistently present
throughout the entire interview [ 21]. Therefore, a multi-level seg-
ment fusion is needed to integrate the segment-based detection
results, forming a more comprehensive and robust subject-level
depression detection framework.
To address these challenges, this paper focuses on the MPDD-
Elderly Track and proposes a Multi-Level Segment Fusion Based on
Adaptive Time-Window Selection (MSF-ATS) method. We classify
the audio and visual feature segments in two time windows accord-
ing to the competition setup. Then, we combine the segment-level
classification results of different features through weighted fusion
to obtain subject-level classifications for each time window. Sub-
sequently, we propose an adaptive time-window selection based
on mean class variance (MCV). By comparing the MCV at differ-
ent time windows, we select the time window with the smallest
variance across the two time window divisions in the competition
achieving more stable detection results. Experimental results show
that our method achieves an average score of 0.8576 on the MPDD
2025 official test set, significantly outperforming the official base-
line score of 0.6675, thus proving the effectiveness of the proposed
method in elderly depression detection tasks.2 Method
The MSF-ATS is shown in Figure 1. The first part is subject-level
classification, which utilizes audio, visual, and textual modalities,
along with multi-level segment fusion to obtain subject-level clas-
sifications for each time window. The second part is adaptive time-
window selection based on MCV, which selects the most suitable
time scale by comparing the MCV at different time windows to
obtain more stable and accurate detection results.
2.1 Subject-Level Classification
Depressive symptoms in clinical interviews typically appear only in
brief segments rather than persisting throughout an entire conver-
sation. To capture these sparse cues and generate clinically mean-
ingful subject-level classification, we design a subject-level classifi-
cation module that aggregates segment-level classifications across
multiple modalities. In common segment-level settings, all audio or
video fragments from the same participant are assigned the same
label [ 8,20]; however, when an explicit identity indicator is ab-
sent, the model may fail to exploit cross-fragment consistency. To
address this, we concatenate a text-derived subject identity embed-
ding to each video or audio feature segment, providing an explicit
affiliation signal that encourages the model to treat segments from
the same participant coherently.
All input features in this module are drawn from the fixed feature
sets provided for the MPDD 2025 Challenge. Audio features include
Wav2Vec [ 19] embeddings and Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients
(MFCCs) [ 17]. Visual features are extracted using DenseNet [ 11],
ResNet [ 9], and OpenFace [ 1]. Text features representing the subject
identity embedding are obtained by encoding subject-level demo-
graphic and clinical attributes with a pretrained RoBERTa [ 14]
model and using the [CLS] token representation.

3.1 Database
The MPDD 2025 [ 7] Challenge hosted at ACM Multimedia 2025 pro-
vides a standardized benchmark to advance research in automated
depression detection. It consists of two tracks: MPDD-Elderly which
focuses on depression detection in elderly subjects and MPDD-
Young which targets younger adults. This paper focuses on MPDD-
Elderly which aims to classify depressive symptoms in older adults
using multimodal behavioral signals and demographic features.
The Challenge consists of three classification levels: binary classi-
fication (normal vs depressed), ternary classification (normal, mildly
depressed, severely depressed), and quinary classification (normal,
mildly depressed, moderately depressed, moderately severe, severe).
The dataset consists of 337 labeled training samples, 227 official
test samples, and an additional subset of 78 early-released test sam-
ples for model selection. Model training is strictly confined to the
training set, while the early-released subset is used for model se-
lection. The final evaluation is performed on the complete official
test set. For clarity, we refer to the early-released test subset as the
validation set throughout the rest of this paper.
Because of privacy and ethical constraints, direct access to raw
audio, video, and textual data is prohibited; consequently, the chal-
lenge organizers supply only a set of pre-extracted multimodal fea-
tures for model development. These features span three complemen-
tary streams: the visual modality is extracted through DenseNet [ 11],
ResNet [ 9], and OpenFace [ 1]. DenseNet and ResNet are convolu-
tional neural networks used for deep facial representation, while
OpenFace captures dynamic facial features such as action units,
gaze direction, and head movements. The audio modality includes
Wav2Vec [ 19] embeddings, MFCCs [ 17] and OpenSMILE [ 6]. Wav2V-
ec is a self-supervised model based on the Transformer architecture
that learns context-aware representations from raw audio signals,
while MFCCs describe the spectral characteristics of audio. OpenS-
MILE is a feature extraction toolkit used to capture prosodic cues,
emotion, and speaker characteristics. The text modality includes
subject-level metadata, such as demographic and clinical attributes,
provided in a structured JSON format. These descriptions are pro-
cessed with a pretrained RoBERTa model, from which the [CLS]
token embedding represents individual characteristics in a fixed-
length vector.
3.2 Experimental setup
The experiments were conducted using the PyTorch framework,
with model training performed using the Adam [ 12] optimizer. In
the first stage, all training and evaluation processes were executed
on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090D GPU. For each unimodal feature,
the training and hyperparameter tuning process was conducted
as follows: First, we performed a comprehensive hyperparameter
search, exploring different learning rates, including 1×10−3,1×
10−4and1×10−5. The weight for the CE loss was fixed at 1.0,
while the weight for the Focal Loss was tuned across different
ranges: from 0.01 to 0.1 (step size 0.001), from 0.1 to 1 (step size
0.1), from 1 to 10 (step size 1) and from 10 to 100 (step size 10).

4 Conclusion
In this paper, we propose the MSF-ATS method, applied to the
MPDD-Elderly track of the MPDD 2025 challenge. To address the
intermittent nature of depressive symptoms during clinical inter-
views, our method first performs segment-level classification on
audio and visual features and aggregates these segment-level classi-
fication results into subject-level classifications to form multi-level
segment fusion. This approach effectively utilizes multimodal in-
formation while considering the intermittent nature of depressive
symptoms at the segment level, generating more stable subject-level
detection. Furthermore, we introduce an adaptive time-window
selection based on mean class variance, allowing the model to dy-
namically adjust the optimal temporal windows based on stability.
Experimental results show that our method achieved an average
score of 0.8576 on the MPDD 2025 official test set, significantly
outperforming the baseline score, thus validating the effectiveness
of our approach. In the future, we will consider integrating more
features to enhance the model’s depression detection capability. Ad-
ditionally, we will investigate the effect of slice duration on depres-
sion detection and optimize the time-window selection mechanism
to advance the development of multimodal depression diagnosis
toward clinical deployment.
